LifeThreads · make the videos with AI

AI Ad Kit

AI makes the people scenes and the voice. The app screens come from real recordings of the app, never from AI. You put the pieces together in CapCut.

How it fits together

  1. Make the people shots

    Paste each shot prompt below into Google Veo, Kling or Runway. Make 2–3 tries per shot and keep the best one.

  2. Make the voice-over

    Paste the script into ElevenLabs, or use CapCut’s text-to-speech. Choose a warm, friendly voice, then one for German.

  3. Add the real app screens

    Use the recordings in build/ad_video/en/ and build/ad_video/de/; the table below says which part to use.

  4. Edit in CapCut

    Shots in order, voice on top, music quiet underneath (about 20 % volume while someone speaks), captions, end card. Export 1080×1920.

Important for every AI shot

When a phone is in the picture, its screen must face away from the camera or be dark. AI draws fake apps; the real app is always cut in from the recording.

V1 · “The moment becomes a memory” (about 22 s)

A real moment, filmed like a movie. A guest takes the photo, the shutter freezes that exact frame, and the photo flies into LifeThreads as a memory with its story. Then it happens again, faster, and every photo ends up on one wall.

The trick: every Veo scene ends on the photo that is in the app

In Google Flow choose Frames to Video, format 9:16, and upload the photo as the last frame (leave the first frame empty). The vertical photos are ready in store_listing_assets/social/ads/veo_photos/lastframe_9x16_*.jpg. Because the clip lands exactly on the app’s photo, the edit can freeze it and fly it into the app without a visible jump.

Scene A · first dance · last frame: lastframe_9x16_first_dance.jpg

Cinematic wedding scene in a rustic wooden barn glowing with thousands of warm string lights, soft haze in the air. Shot on 35 mm film look, 24 fps, slow motion, shallow depth of field, gentle anamorphic lens flares. The bride and groom dance their first dance in the middle of the room while the guests form a circle around them, clapping and swaying, golden confetti drifting slowly through the light. The camera glides in a slow arc around the couple. The bride laughs and leans her head against the groom. At the end the camera comes to rest in front of them and the movement slows to stillness, landing exactly on the final frame. Sound: soft crowd murmur, laughter, rustle of the dress, and one crisp phone camera shutter click on the very last frame. No music, no text, no logos.

Scene B · first bike · last frame: lastframe_9x16_first_bike.jpg

Cinematic autumn forest path at golden hour, warm sun rays through orange and red leaves, 35 mm film look, slow motion, shallow depth of field. A little boy in a blue helmet pedals his small blue bike on his own for the very first time, wobbling and then finding his balance. His father runs behind him, lets go and throws his arms wide, laughing and cheering. Leaves swirl up behind the wheels. The camera tracks backwards in front of them, then slows to a stop, landing exactly on the final frame. Sound: crunching leaves, the boy’s delighted laugh, the father cheering, one phone camera shutter click on the very last frame. No music, no text, no logos.

Scene C · garden dinner · last frame: lastframe_9x16_garden_dinner.jpg

Cinematic summer evening in a garden, a long wooden table under string lights and glowing lanterns, three generations of one family at dinner, candles and wine glasses. 35 mm film look, shallow depth of field, warm dusk light. Slow dolly shot along the table past laughing faces and passing hands; at the far end the grandfather raises his glass and everyone toasts him. The camera settles and the moment slows to stillness, landing exactly on the final frame. Sound: clinking glasses, laughter, a few words of a toast, one phone camera shutter click on the very last frame. No music, no text, no logos.

If a clip doesn’t land cleanly on the photo, generate it again; that last frame is what makes the transition work. Watch hands and faces in slow motion closely.

Voice-over scripts

Voice: warm, friendly, smiling, like talking to a friend. Not like a radio ad. In ElevenLabs set Stability about 40 % and Style about 30 %.

V2 · Presenter prompt

For Arcads, Creatify or HeyGen: pick a presenter and paste the V2 script. Then put the app recording (scenes “wall”, “memory”, “story”) on screen while they talk.

Presenter look

A friendly woman in her early thirties sits on a cozy sofa at home in natural daylight, casual sweater, holding a phone, talking straight to the camera like recording a story for friends. Vertical 9:16, handheld selfie framing.

Before you post

  • Watch every AI shot closely: hands, fingers, faces and text often come out wrong. Cut those frames.
  • Mark the ad as AI-generated where the platform asks (TikTok and Instagram have a label for it).
  • Keep captions out of the top 250 px, the bottom 400 px and the right 130 px of the frame.
  • Music: the app’s own tracks are CC0 and free for ads, for example “The Good Times” or “Morning Coffee” by HoliznaCC0.
